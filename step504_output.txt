(3) THE TWO CONDITIONS PULL IN OPPOSITE DIRECTIONS. THE CLOSED FORM.
============================================================================================================

  A1 says: the reference must cover >= 98% of the pooled calendar, 2021-01-01 -> 2025-06-15.
     => its window must START at or before 2021-02-02, i.e. essentially at the pooled start.
  A2 says: the same reference must cover >= 98% of the ROW's own fenced window,
     which for every one of these four ENDS LATER than any 2021-starting tape's fence does.

  Under a fence that keeps a fixed 80% of a window, a reference that starts EARLIER than
  the row also fences EARLIER than the row, by exactly 20% of the head start. A2 therefore
  tolerates a lead of at most (1-0.98)*0.80/(1-0.80) = 8% of the row's remaining window.

  Evaluated at TODAY's corpus boundary, 2026-07-26. The allowance grows with the boundary, which is
  what part (4) sweeps.

     row    row starts    A2 lead budget  ref must start  youngest dense ref  its start    verdict
     --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
     XRP    2024-01-01              75 d   2023-10-18   DOT                 2023-08-18   NO SUCH REFERENCE - all are older
     DOT    2023-08-18              86 d   2023-05-24   AVAX                2021-11-18   NO SUCH REFERENCE - all are older
     AVAX   2021-11-18             137 d   2021-07-04   DOT                 2023-08-18   satisfiable
     ADA    2026-02-13              13 d   2026-01-30   DOT                 2023-08-18   NO SUCH REFERENCE - all are older

  And the reference that satisfies A2 must ALSO satisfy A1, which pins it to the 2021 start.
  For these four rows the two demands name disjoint sets of start dates TODAY, and the gap
  closes only as slowly as the lead budget grows - 8 days a year per 100 days of new tape.

(5) THE HAZARD THE ITEM WARNED ABOUT IS BIGGER THAN THE ITEM THOUGHT: FENCES ARE NOT DATES
============================================================================================================

  `cut80` recomputes the boundary from whatever span the file has TODAY. Every published
  sealed-slice boundary in this log is therefore a FUNCTION OF THE FILE, not a date, and moves
  0.8 days for every day of tape appended. 53 days of real bars exist between the corpus boundary
  and today (2026-09-17) and were deliberately not fetched. Had they been:

  This is not hypothetical bookkeeping. R492 spent LINK's final 20% and published XRP's as
  INTACT, both as DATE RANGES. Appending seven weeks of real tape would silently redefine both
  of those ranges - part of what R492 read as LINK's SEALED slice would become readable, and
  part of what it read as XRP's TRAIN/VAL would become XRP's sealed slice. The desk's last
  clean window is defined by a formula that any future data pull moves. That is a bookkeeping
  defect, it is live today, and it is why this round wrote nothing.

  WHAT IS NEW: the four unplaced rows are not waiting on data. Three routes exist and this
  round deliberately takes NONE of them, because choosing one after seeing which rows it
  would place is the exact failure mode item 38 was opened to prevent:
    (a) FENCE SHAPE. A fence that keeps a fixed NUMBER of trailing days instead of a fixed
        proportion makes every tape's admissible region end on the same date, and A2 becomes
        satisfiable. It also changes the size of every sealed slice in this log.
    (b) POOLED CALENDAR. A1 is measured against BTC's fenced window because R502 chose it and
        R503 inherited it. A pooled calendar built from the INTERSECTION of the rows being
        ranked is a different and defensible question that has never been asked.
    (c) ACCEPT IT. Publish {PAXG, SOL, XRP} unordered permanently and stop spending rounds on
        the cell. The ranking's job is to point at a slice; it already points at three.

  Any of the three must be pre-registered like R503's rule was, with the decision fixed before
  the resulting order is computed. That is a queue item, not a footnote to this one.
