5. THE DAY-BOUNDARY ARTEFACT ON CRYPTO, measured
   tjr_bot.run_day closes anything still open when a UTC day's bars run out.
   The live path does not: manage_step holds through the boundary. So these
   exits are truncations, and they truncate winners while losers still reach
   their stops.
   trades that ended that way   1,762 of 8,139  (21.6%)
   what they made               $+544,931   (51.6% of them won, average +0.218 times what was risked)
   crypto as measured            8,139 trades   39.0% won   -0.236x risked  $    -941,743
   crypto with them removed      6,377 trades   35.5% won   -0.361x risked  $  -1,486,673
   This is a SENSITIVITY CHECK, not the headline. Removing them drops real
   trades the bot really took.
   AND IT DOES NOT POINT THE WAY THE ARTEFACT WAS ASSUMED TO. Those trades were
   AHEAD at the boundary — 51.6% of them were in profit when the day's bars ran
   out — so deleting them deletes a winning subset and the result gets WORSE,
   not better. What the artefact actually does is CAP those winners at whatever
   they had at 00:00 UTC when the live path would have run them on to a target
   or a stop. The true cost therefore sits between the two lines above and
   cannot be settled by deleting anything. Settling it needs a replay that
   walks through the boundary, which this round was not allowed to build.

6. THE AVERAGE RESULT, CHECKED FOR OUTLIERS
   A few crypto trades sit on a stop a fraction of a cent wide. The set size
   then buys an enormous position against a few dollars of intended loss, and
   the result as a MULTIPLE of that few dollars goes to -225. The dollars are
   unaffected — it is the ratio that blows up — but the mean multiple is not
   safe on its own, so here it is beside its median and its 1%-trimmed mean.
