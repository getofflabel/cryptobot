The Playbook

Everything we believe, how we decide, what we trade, and where we honestly stand. Written for Wallace Chen · Version 2 · 24 July 2026

1. The goal

Get the practice account to $3,000, then keep compounding. That is the target this whole machine points at. It starts from about $1,350 today.

The way we get there is not one big win. It is many ordinary trades, banked and re-bet. Wallace's own words, and this is the mathematics of the whole project:

"Money doesn't have to be made in the big trades. It could be made in the small trades. It's compounding at the end of the day. You could be in and out within an hour."

A machine that nets even a fraction of a percent a day, consistently, compounds to a large number in a year. A machine that waits for perfect trades makes twenty attempts a year and learns nothing. We are building the first kind.

How we count the money honestly

The bot's score starts at zero from the moment it began trading its own signals. Any profit that was in the account before that is Wallace's own trading and is never counted as the machine's performance. When this document says the machine made or lost money, it means trades the machine decided and placed itself. Paper trades on oil and the S&P count too, because the machine decided, sized, executed and exited them at real prices, only the ledger holding the dollars is internal.

2. How we operate: the attitude

We do not say something can't be done.

"There's no point of saying this won't work. Our job is to make it work. If there's people doing it, it can work."

If a real person trades a style profitably, that style is possible, and any failure we get is a failure of our version of it. The correct response to a bad test result is "what did we get wrong," never "it can't be done." Defeatist framing is banned from this project.

Profitable traders are profitable for a reason.

When we study a trader like TJR, Alex Gonzalez or PB Blake and our coded version of their method loses money, the honest conclusion is that our formalisation is incomplete, not that they are lying. The job is to find the piece we failed to capture: the context they read, the setups they skipped, the judgment that lives on the chart and not in the words.

Frequency is a symptom of knowledge, not a target.

A trader who knows one setup waits weeks. A trader who knows twenty finds several a day in the same market. So when the machine is quiet, the diagnosis is "it does not know enough yet," and the fix is more validated tools, never forcing trades.

Speed of learning beats caution.

"I'd rather you take ten to twenty trades tomorrow and have most of them lose, than take one trade or none. You can't learn from one trade a day."

The practice account is tuition, not a trophy. Trading a lot, honestly recorded, is how the machine builds the judgment to become selective later.

The trap we must avoid: mismatched timeframes

An expensive mistake we made and fixed: justifying a trade on the daily chart while placing a stop and target sized for a two-hour scalp. Those are two different trades wearing one ticket. If the reason is the daily chart, the trade needs days of room. If the stop is 1% away, then the intraday chart must be what justified it.

4. The trader's toolkit, and what actually tested true

These are the tools Wallace uses to read a chart. Every one has now been coded and tested on years of real data. This is what we found.

ToolWhat it means in plain wordsWhat the data said
Liquidity and sweepsClusters of equal highs or lows where stops pile up. A "sweep" is price raiding that pool then snapping back.Useless as a signal on its own. Valuable as a vote alongside others. Confirmed: 62-89% of sharp breaks snap back, so the reclaim is the tradeable side, not the break.
Break of structureThe chain of higher highs and higher lows breaking character.Works, and is the backbone of our best crypto edge, but only with agreement from other tools.
Fair value gapsThree-candle imbalances where price moved so fast it left a hole.No edge alone. Useful as a supporting vote.
Equilibrium (premium/discount)The midpoint of the current range: buy the lower half, sell the upper half.No edge alone. Good as a filter on top of a real signal.
Fibonacci retracementThe 0.618 to 0.79 pullback zone of a move.No edge alone in our tests. Contributes as a vote.
ConfluenceOnly trade when several tools agree.This is the finding. A structure break with zero agreement loses money. With two or more agreeing, it was strongly profitable through the hardest recent market. Wallace's central claim tested true.
Candle patternsPin bars, engulfing candles, inside bars. Zero survivors in 112 tested versions on Bitcoin. Adding "context" made them worse, not better.
Order blocksThe last opposing candle before a strong move.Zero survivors in 64 versions.
DivergencePrice and momentum disagreeing.The textbook reversal version is noise. The continuation version (hidden divergence) passed its sealed test and is now live.

The lesson underneath the table: individually, most of these tools are not signals. Together, they are a committee, and the committee has an edge. That is why our best crypto strategy requires a structure break plus at least two agreeing conditions.

The selectivity sweet spot

We measured both failure modes. Too little confirmation and you trade noise: a "reversal candle" on the 15-minute chart appears on roughly half of all bars. Too much and you never trade: one popular published system, tested honestly across 30 years, produced about one trade every two years. The target is the middle: enough agreement to have an edge, few enough conditions to actually fire.

5. Sessions and timeframes

Sessions

Even though crypto never closes, the world's money keeps office hours, and it shows.

  • Asia (00:00-07:00 UTC): quieter, sets the overnight range.
  • London (07:00-13:00 UTC): the first real volume of the day.
  • New York (13:00-21:00 UTC): the heaviest hours; US equity and futures markets are open inside this window.
  • Weekend: crypto keeps trading with thinner liquidity, which changes what works.

Measured, not assumed: adding the session lens to our research tripled the rate at which previously-dead tools showed a real edge. Our best structure strategy concentrates its profit in the New York session. Some tools only work in Asian hours; one only works on weekends. Session is now a first-class part of how the machine reads a situation.

Timeframes: what each chart is for

ChartIts job
Daily / 4-hourContext and direction only. Never the justification for a quick trade.
1-hourThe setup: where a tradeable situation is identified.
15-minute / 5-minuteThe trigger: precise entry timing, so the stop can be tight and the payoff large. This is the timeframe the machine is being grown into.
VolatilityPermission. Fast trades need a market that is moving. In dead tape, only the strongest setups may fire.

There is no clock in crypto, but there is a clock on a thesis

Crypto trades 24/7, so we never close a position because "the market is closing." But some strategies do carry a time limit, and it is a limit on the idea, not the market. A news reaction is spent within about a day; after that, whatever price does is unrelated to the reason we entered, so we leave.

6. Reading the day before trading it

A good trader reads the situation first and then picks the tool. Our machine is being built to do the same thing, and the model for it is the kind of daily note Wallace's alpha group writes:

"Bitcoin, provided it stays above 64K, is set for probably mid-range chop. Monday's low to Tuesday's high is 63.7 up to 67K, call it a 3K weekly range, and we're trading 65 to 65.5, mid-range. Better trades present the closer we get to 64K or 67K. For now, let it chop."

Everything in that paragraph is now computed automatically every day and stored as the machine's memory of the week: the weekly range, where price sits inside it, whether we are at an edge or stuck mid-range, which market is stronger, what the volatility regime is, plus the cross-market context (the S&P's key level, and the price gap between Brent and American oil, which widens under global stress).

Two rules come out of that read, and they are the machine's version of "let it chop":

  • Mid-range and quiet: only A-grade setups. The machine does not gamble in dead tape.
  • At the edges of the range: pay attention. That is where trades present.

The bigger goal here is what we call reading the scenario. Instead of a rack of separate tripwires each waiting for its own signal, the machine classifies the day (trending or ranging, quiet or violent, news in the air or not, crowd leaning long or short, which session) and then uses the tools that have historically earned money in that specific situation. We have the map: for example, one short-selling tool has made money in exactly one condition, violent markets with the crowd heavily long, and shows no evidence of working anywhere else. Turning that map into the machine's live decision-making is the current major project.

7. What we believe about markets

  • Most trading ideas do not work. We have tested roughly two thousand versions. The large majority lost money after costs. Any process that does not throw away most of its ideas is fooling itself.
  • Costs decide more than people think. A round trip on our exchange costs about 0.12%. If you are hunting a 0.2% move, most of your profit is gone before you start. This killed six families of fast-trading ideas. The fix is not "don't trade fast," it is "hunt moves much bigger than the cost of hunting them." Fast entry, full-sized target.
  • An edge is small and repeatable. Most profitable traders win 40-55% of the time and make money because winners are bigger than losers. We measure dollars per trade after costs, never win rate alone.
  • A tested idea can go stale. One of our own live strategies quietly started losing money as the market calmed. We caught it, fixed it, and made re-testing live strategies permanent. Passing a test buys confidence, not immortality.
  • The right tool depends on the situation. See section 6.

8. How we prove something works

We split history into three parts, in time order:

WindowShareWhat it is for
TrainingFirst 60%Where we may tune and experiment freely.
ValidationNext 20%A check on data we did not tune on. Ideas that only worked in training die here.
Sealed testFinal 20%Untouched history, looked at once. The closest thing to having traded it live in the past.

Minimum bars: at least 30 trades in training and 8 in validation, positive dollars per trade after all costs in both, and then one look at the sealed test. Every look we have ever spent is counted (about 25 so far) because repeated peeking turns an honest test into a fitted one.

The replay exam. Beyond single ideas, we drop the entire current machine into the past and let it trade forward bar by bar with no knowledge of the future. That is the closest honest answer to "would this have made money." Results in section 12.

The graveyard. Every failed idea is written down with the reason. It stops us re-testing dead ends and it tells us what kind of idea tends to fail here. Important caveat, per section 2: a failed test of someone's published method means our version failed, and the first question is always what we mis-coded.

9. What we trade, market by market

Crypto (Bitcoin, Ethereum, Solana)

Character: never closes, reacts to news within minutes, volatility shrinking year over year, and the three coins move together, so we treat them as one family and never hold opposing bets across them.

What works: trading the direction of the first hour after a market-moving headline; trend-following with a strict "the market is actually moving" filter; buying panic inside an uptrend but only after the selling visibly stops; hidden (continuation) divergence on the 4-hour chart; and a structure break backed by at least two agreeing conditions.

What does not: standing short strategies (five burials), chasing breakdowns (six), candle patterns, order blocks, textbook reversal divergence, and small-profit scalping (six, all killed by fees).

Gold

Character: calmer than crypto so crypto settings never transfer; a long-running uptrend that punishes short-sellers; a rhythm tied to London and New York. We trade it on our exchange through a gold-backed token, the only gold instrument the practice venue offers, and it is now placing real orders there.

What works: buying a break above the highest price of the last 20 days, and then holding while a stop trails up behind the structure instead of taking a fixed profit. That exit change alone made roughly four times the money on identical entries.

What does not: buying dips against the trend, short-selling, and trading the London/New York opens as a standalone signal.

The S&P 500

Character: the most orderly market we have studied. Upward drift, session-bound, and it recovers from panics more reliably than anything else. Our news feed does not move it.

What works: buying an extreme multi-day flush while the index is still above its 200-day average, and holding until it normalises. Profitable in 10 of the last 11 years, winning about 70% of the time. The cleanest edge we own.

What does not: momentum and trend-chasing (zero survivors in 24 versions), gap trading, and the famous overnight drift, which is real but too small to beat costs.

Oil

Where we honestly stand: this is our weakest market. We do not yet have a single strategy tested and proven on oil itself. What runs there are strategies proven on gold and the index, borrowed on an untested assumption. Oil gets its own research program, the same treatment the S&P received.

What we do know: it trends hard on geopolitics, and the price gap between Brent and American oil widens under global stress. We track that gap daily; it is currently in the top 10% of its recent range.

First result: on 24 July the oil system took its first trade and closed it at target for +$58.39 in about two hours, the machine's first winning closed trade.

Why oil and the S&P trade on paper

Not by choice. Our exchange does not offer them, and every practice broker we tried (Alpaca, Capital.com, OANDA, TradingView's simulator) either failed or demanded identity verification. So those two markets trade against real prices on an internal ledger, are shown on the dashboard exactly like real positions, and count in the statistics. A daily check watches for the day our exchange lists them, and they switch to real orders immediately when it does.

10. How a trade happens, start to finish

  1. Read the day. Weekly range, position within it, volatility, session, crowd positioning.
  2. Scan. Every two hours across all six markets, plus continuous watching by the specialist systems (a headline, a breakout, a divergence).
  3. Score. Each market gets a number built from named components (daily trend, hourly trend, breakout proximity, washout, unusual volume, funding, momentum). Every component that fires is recorded, so every trade can be explained in plain words afterwards.
  4. Check the guards. Is another system already in this symbol? Would this contradict our existing crypto position? Did we just get stopped out of this exact trade in the last six hours without the signal improving? Is the market too quiet for this kind of trade? Any failure and we pass, and the pass is logged with its reason.
  5. Size by risk. Decide the risk (2% of the ledger), place the stop where the chart says it belongs, and let position size fall out of those two numbers. Never the reverse.
  6. Enter with one market order. No resting limit orders, no sequences of small orders that can jam halfway.
  7. Protect on the exchange. The stop goes on the exchange immediately, so it works even if our computer dies. Isolated margin, so this trade can only ever cost its own margin.
  8. Manage. Every cycle, verify the protection is still live and re-place it if it vanished. Trailing stops move up, never down.
  9. Exit at the stop, the target, a structure break, or the thesis's time limit.
  10. Log and learn. Written into the daybook with reasoning, outcome and hold time. Setup types that keep losing get switched off automatically. Trades we passed are also logged and scored the next day, so every protective rule has to prove it saves more than it costs.

11. Exits: where the profit actually comes from

Exits turned out to matter as much as entries, and Wallace's instinct here changed the machine twice.

  • A target should come from the chart, not from a percentage. The old strategies used fixed targets like "+2.4%," chosen before anyone looked at the situation. Wallace called that out, and testing agreed: on gold, replacing the fixed exit with one that follows the chart's structure made about four times the money. On the news strategy, the fixed target had gone stale and was losing about $15 a trade; the structure-based exit turned it positive.
  • Where to put a target. Not at the obvious level, just in front of it. For a short, that means above the demand zone where buyers previously stepped in, so we get filled before the crowd waiting there. Never at the first bounce level, and never inside empty space when there is a real level just beyond it.
  • Banking half at a target, then trailing the rest, sounds better than it tested. On the news strategy it lost to pure trailing, because 70% of those trades never reach a target at all and the ones that do tend to keep running.
  • Stops move toward profit or they do not move. Widening a stop because a trade is losing is how small losses become large ones.

13. The rules we never break

1. Never trade an idea that has not passed the wall.

2. Every position has a stop on the exchange, always.

3. Enter with market orders only, never resting limit orders.

4. One thesis on crypto at a time. Never long one coin and short another.

5. Isolated margin, so one accident cannot reach the rest of the account.

6. Risk first, size second.

7. Never move a stop away from price.

8. The sealed test gets looked at once, and every look is counted.

9. Report the truth immediately, especially when it is bad.

10. Speak in reasons, not internal names.

16. What we are still missing

  • A proven fast-entry method. Wallace's own timeframe. The obstacle was never the timeframe, it was that early attempts hunted moves too small to survive fees. Current work aims at 5 and 15-minute entries into full-sized moves.
  • The scenario brain, live. We have the map of which tool works in which situation. It is not yet driving the machine's decisions.
  • A real venue for oil and the S&P.
  • An oil-specific edge.
  • A green week. The only number that settles the argument.

17. The short version

We are getting a practice account to $3,000 by trading many ordinary trades well, not by waiting for perfect ones. We trade Wallace's way: fast entries, real moves, active most days, explained in plain words.

We only trade what we have proven on history the idea never saw, and we throw away almost everything. We size by risk, protect every position on the exchange, never bet against ourselves, and keep an honest record including the trades we passed on.

When something we believed turns out wrong, we say so the same day and fix it. When a method works for a real trader and fails for us, we assume we coded it wrong and go find out why. We do not say it cannot be done.

Right now: five proven strategies, a machine that works, a decade-long test saying it would have made money, and a live record with one win in it. The job is to turn that into a green week, without breaking any of the sentences above.
