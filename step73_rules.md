## 1. The core mechanical system — market structure + break-and-retest

His system (spread across chapters 17/19/20/21, restated consistently in
the live chapters) reduces to ONE mechanism used for BOTH "reversal" and
"trend continuation" trades — they are the same trigger, differing only
in whether the break agrees with or reverses the prevailing higher-
timeframe trend:

**STEP 1 — top-down structure & bias.**
"This market is bullish because it is consisted of higher highs and
higher lows" / "a bearish market is equivalent to lower low and lower
high." MECHANICAL, with one explicit, repeated exclusion: **"do not take
the wicks into account"** — structure is built from candle BODIES only
("at no point are we including wicks here"). A break of structure /
trend flip is a body **close** beyond the prior confirmed swing extreme:
"once we body close above the higher high or the higher low, the market
has shifted." Two consecutive timeframes (weekly+daily, or daily+4H)
must show the SAME bias before a pair is even watchlist-worthy: "you
need two consecutive time frames in sync." Timeframe set used
throughout: "I only use the weekly, the daily, the 4 hour, the 2 hour,
the 1 hour, the 30 [minute], and the 15 minute."

**"Snake trick" — swing-point identification.** Starting from a new
extreme, scan backward candle-by-candle until the first clear reversal
("turn") — that point becomes the paired swing low (uptrend) or swing
high (downtrend). MECHANICAL INTENT, DISCRETIONARY IN PRACTICE: no
numeric threshold is ever given for what counts as a valid "turn." His
own words: **"it needs to be a very sharp, clean turning point... that
is what's going to be considered a valid structure point"** — and he
repeatedly overrides his own calls by eye on similar-looking candles
("this one candlestick right here to me is simply not strong enough").
He states outright the limit of formalizing this: "there's never going
to be like a proper black or white textbook."

**STEP 2 — area of interest (AOI).** AOI = his umbrella term for
support/resistance/supply-demand/order-block ("all the same"). MECHANICAL
where stated: drawn ONLY on weekly and daily ("we only look for an area
of interest on the weekly and on the daily," never 4H or lower); minimum
**3 touches** (body-based rejections, not wicks); zone size bounds
**5-60 pips, "sweet spot" 20-35 pips**; must sit strictly inside the
current HH/HL (or LH/LL) range. DISCRETIONARY IN PRACTICE: whether a
"touch" is clean enough to count is repeatedly overridden by eye ("I
would not count that one because the candlestick isn't that clean"), and
which of several overlapping AOIs to prioritize is a manual judgment
call, not a rule.

**STEP 3 — break and retest (the actual entry trigger, both
"reversal" and "trend continuation" chapters describe the SAME
mechanism).** Price must (a) body-close beyond the AOI's internal
structure point (the break — with the trend = continuation, against the
trend = reversal/CHoCH), then (b) retrace back to retest that broken
level, then (c) show a REVERSAL/CONTINUATION CANDLESTICK PATTERN at the
retest before entry is valid: "you can only enter properly on the retest
and then you need proper body rejections." Entry timing: either right at
the confirmed breakout close (he says he does this "maybe 30%" of the
time) or — his stated preference — waiting for the retest+candle
confirmation.

**Named candlestick confirmation patterns** (all must occur AT the AOI
to count — "they need to happen at a key area of interest," the same
shape elsewhere on the chart is explicitly disregarded):
- **Engulfing candle** — his own, stricter-than-textbook definition:
  "needs to engulf the last candlestick and then the previous one
  minimum" — i.e. the confirmation candle's body must engulf the prior
  TWO candles' bodies, not just one.
- **Morning star / evening star** — his stated favorite ("end all be
  all"): a doji/hammer/spinning-top candle immediately followed by an
  engulfing candle that eats the prior two candle bodies. Numerically
  this REDUCES to the same engulf-prior-two-bodies test above (the small
  "indecision" candle is simply one of the two candles being engulfed) —
  no separate doji/small-body ratio is ever given a number.
- Hammer / inverted hammer / dragonfly doji: rejection-wick variants,
  treated by him as interchangeable ("dogeis"), same "no wick" vs. "wick
  fill" logic, again no numeric wick:body ratio stated.
- **Explicitly dismissed by name**: piercing line ("I don't even know
  what this is... they just made up this formation"), three white
  soldiers/three black crows/three-river ("a waste of time").

**STOP.** Beyond the invalidating wick/swing point that would negate the
setup, plus an unstated buffer. Only one numeric worked example exists
("stop loss anywhere from 10 to 15 pips above this wick... 20 23 pips"
total) — no general pip/ATR/percentage formula is given anywhere in the
transcript. MECHANICAL INTENT (beyond the nearest opposing swing point),
DISCRETIONARY EXACT DISTANCE.

**TARGET.** Stated floor: **"a minimum of a one to two risk-to-reward...
always aim for the trade to have a potential of a one to 4."** He
explicitly does NOT cap trades at 1:2 — the decision to keep holding past
1:2 is framed as a live, subjective call ("you make the decision to
determine if you want to continue to hold or not"), not a rule. Realized
examples across the live chapters range widely: 1:2.7, 1:3.4, 1:5,
even one outlier at "1 to 11 risk-to-reward." DISCRETIONARY beyond the
stated 1:2 floor / 1:4 "potential" anchor points.

**RISK PER TRADE.** No general formula, percentage, or lot-size equation
is stated anywhere in the teaching chapters (21/22) — confirmed by an
exhaustive keyword search for "%", "percent", "leverage", "lot size",
"drawdown" across both chapters; the only hits are incidental, non-
sizing uses of "%". In the live "$100 to a million" challenge he DOES
narrate a risk-percentage schedule, but it is explicitly gut-feel, not a
formula, and starts extremely aggressive: **trade 1 = 100% of account
risked**, stepping down over weeks to "never went below 35%. Maybe... 30%,
27%, depending on the type of trade." This is a marketing-stunt risk
profile (repeated account-blow-up-and-restart dynamics, by his own
admission), explicitly NOT representative of the prudent-sizing framing
his "Risk Management" chapter title implies, and NOT usable as a
position-sizing rule for any serious backtest. UNSPECIFIED / not
mechanizable from this source — flagged, not guessed, exactly like

## 2. Trend continuation vs. reversal — the SAME mechanism, different gate

Chapter 21 ("Trend Continuation") and chapter 20 ("Reversal Patterns")
both reduce to break -> retest -> engulf-prior-2-candles confirmation.
The only difference: a CONTINUATION trade's break agrees with the
already-prevailing chain state (a "cont_long"/"cont_short" event in this
repo's step56_smc_toolkit's bos_chain() vocabulary); a REVERSAL trade's
break is the FIRST break against the prevailing chain (a "choch_long"/
"choch_short" event) — the classic BOS-continuation vs. CHoCH split this
repo has formalized before (step56). This is stated explicitly by his
own worked live example, where he waits for the SAME break-retest-
engulf sequence whether he is fading a top (reversal) or riding a
pullback (continuation): "I want the market to show me its hand first."
