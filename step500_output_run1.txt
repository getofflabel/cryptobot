(3) CAN A TENTH-FULL TAPE CARRY A COORDINATE AT ALL?  - the question the item says decides
    how much the corrected number is worth
============================================================================================================
This is not answered by opinion. Take instruments whose 1-minute tape IS dense, measure the
gap-clean coordinate on the full tape (the truth), then THIN THEM TO PAXG'S OWN PRESENCE
PATTERN and re-measure with the identical code. If the thinned read comes back at the truth,
a tenth-full tape carries the coordinate. If it comes back HIGH, PAXG's 0.0791% is an
estimate of its ACTIVE minutes, not of its average minute, and the multiple is inflated.

Two thinnings, because they answer different questions:
  PAXG-MASK  - keep only the (day, minute-of-day) cells PAXG itself has a bar in. This is
               thin AND selective: gold on a crypto venue prints when it trades.
  RANDOM     - keep the same NUMBER of bars per day, chosen uniformly at random (seed 500).
               Thin but NOT selective. The gap between the two columns is the selection
               effect; the gap between RANDOM and FULL is the thinness effect.
